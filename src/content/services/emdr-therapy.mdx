## Are Painful Memories Keeping You Stuck In Survival Mode?


You don’t have to be experiencing obvious flashbacks or have a PTSD diagnosis to feel the lingering effects of something traumatic you might have experienced. An accident, assault, loss, frightening medical experience, relationship wound, or other distressing event can sometimes continue shaping how you feel, react, and see yourself and the world long after it happened.

Perhaps you’re struggling with:

-   **Memories or reminders that still hit hard:** intrusive thoughts, nightmares, or sudden physical and emotional reactions to triggers.
-   **Overwhelming emotions:** Anxiety, fear, hypervigilance, irritability, sadness, or numbness, leading to patterns of isolation and avoidance. 
-   **Somatic symptoms:** Unexplained muscle tension, sleep problems, chronic fatigue, racing heart, or panic attacks that surface out of nowhere.
-   **Low self-worth and negative self-talk:** feelings of guilt, shame, or self-blame—or self-defeating thoughts, like *I’m not safe*, *I’m powerless*, or *I’m not good enough*
-   **Negative thoughts about the world around you:** feeling unsafe all the time and that none is trustworthy.

### The Pain From The Past Likes To Hide In The Shadows Of The Present

Even when you know on a rational level that you should feel safe, relaxed, or happy, the pain of the past can hijack your mind and body in powerful ways.

Perhaps you avoid people, places, conversations, or experiences that stir something up—or maybe you exercise daily, throw yourself into work, or drink more than you should as a distraction. On the other hand, the fear, self-doubt, and hopelessness may have become so familiar that you don’t immediately connect the way you feel today with something that happened months or years ago.

Instead of constantly trying to negotiate with thoughts and feelings that don’t respond to reason, EMDR fundamentally transforms the way you think and respond to stressors. Our EMDR counselors at Trauma Healing Therapy are here to help you heal and resolve the thoughts, emotions, sensations, and reactions connected to your past traumatic or stressful experiences, so the past can finally begin to feel more like the past.

## Trauma Responses Are The Brain’s Attempt To Protect You

Trauma symptoms aren’t a sign that you’re weak or somehow failing to recover. After something overwhelming or threatening happens, your brain and body naturally prioritize survival. Sometimes, trauma spawns powerful emotions, physical sensations, beliefs, and protective responses meant to help you recognize similar threats in the future.

Often, these reactions gradually settle as the person processes their experience. For some, however, reminders of the past continue to trigger that alarm system months or even years later.

### Why EMDR Treatment Instead Of Traditional Talk Therapy?


When we feel hurt, scared, or threatened, our brain goes into survival mode, bypassing logic and engaging the primal part of the brain where our animal instinct typically lies dormant.

That’s why knowing intellectually that you’re safe doesn’t always make you *feel* safe. That’s also why talk therapy struggles to resolve deep emotional and psychological wounds caused by a traumatic experience. 

A sound, place, argument, or physical sensation—even ordinary situations—can trigger a fight-or-flight response that is nearly impossible to reason with because it reminds you of *that very traumatic experience 10 years ago.* That doesn’t mean your brain is permanently stuck or damaged; it’s just protecting you the only way it knows how.

Unlike talk therapy, EMDR works on a neurological level. Rather than trying to change how you think or feel, EMDR helps you process painful traumatic memories so your brain can finally file them where they belong—in the past. This shifts how you relate to those memories and the things that trigger them, deactivating old survival responses and loosening the past’s grip on the present.

## EMDR Therapy Can Help You Process What The Past Left Behind

Talking about trauma can feel deeply unsettling—especially if you’ve spent years trying to forget the past. And talking alone, without actually reprocessing the memory, can sometimes re-traumatize clients rather than help them heal. That’s why our therapists at Trauma Healing Therapy prioritize building an atmosphere of safety, trust, and genuine understanding before ever moving into the EMDR process itself.

EMDR isn’t about forcing you into painful memories before you’re ready. Rather, it provides a gradual, structured way to work with unresolved experiences while respecting your pace, needs, and comfort level throughout the process. While you will still need to talk to your EMDR therapist, this specific technique (EMDR) reduces the re-traumatization that might happen when talking about a traumatic memory.

## What To Expect From EMDR Treatment

First, we’ll spend time familiarizing ourselves with your history, current symptoms, and what you hope will change. Your EMDR therapist will also help you develop grounding and emotional regulation strategies for managing anything distressing that may come up between or during sessions.

Although talk therapy has its limitations, exploration and discussion are crucial for establishing trust, identifying experiences to target, and making sure you feel prepared for EMDR treatment. If you aren’t ready to talk about something, we don’t need to rush. Trauma often robs us of our voice as well as our sense of safety and power. Our goal is to help you reclaim everything it took from you—and more.

### How EMDR Helps Change Your Response To Painful Memories

Understanding your trauma and story with talk therapy can be enormously valuable, but insight alone doesn’t always change how a painful memory feels. You may know you’re safe and still have a reminder or trigger send you spiraling right back into the past.

This is where the “reprocessing” part of **Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing** therapy becomes important. During EMDR, you’ll briefly approach aspects of a distressing memory while engaging in bilateral stimulation, such as guided eye movements.

EMDR therapy uses these eye movements—similar to the rapid eye movements that occur during REM sleep—to make distressing memories feel less vivid and emotionally intense. Although scientists are still studying exactly how EMDR produces these changes, the results are undeniable, particularly in cases of trauma and PTSD.

Rather than erasing the experience, reprocessing helps the memory become less connected to the intense thoughts, emotions, bodily sensations, and survival responses that once accompanied it. In other words, your brain can begin responding to what happened as something from your past instead of repeatedly sounding the alarm in your present.

### Healing Doesn’t Require Forgetting What Happened

EMDR can’t erase the past, but it can help reduce the distress associated with an experience so remembering it doesn’t continue to make you feel as though you’re back inside it.

As the past becomes less intrusive, you’ll find more room for safety, choice, connection, and confidence in the present. Although what happened remains part of your narrative, EMDR neutralizes its ability to define how your story unfolds from here.

## Questions You May Have About EMDR Therapy…

</div>

<Faq question="Do people ever experience side effects from EMDR treatment?">

Reprocessing difficult experiences can temporarily bring up strong emotions, physical sensations, dreams, or related memories. That’s one reason preparation is an important part of EMDR. Before reprocessing, we’ll practice grounding, self-care, and regulation strategies to help you manage distress if it arises.

EMDR isn’t an endurance test, and faster isn’t necessarily better. Your EMDR counselor can help you move at a pace that respects your goals, readiness, and limitations throughout the process.

</Faq>

<Faq question="What if therapy hasn't helped me before?">

We understand how discouraging that experience must be, but countless factors can influence outcomes. Most of the time, not finding relief or missing your goals comes down to a poor match in therapeutic approach or the therapist you’re working with.

EMDR’s eight-step process takes everything into account: preparation, pacing, therapeutic fit, client goals, and professional expertise. Often considered the frontline treatment for trauma and PTSD by organizations like the US Department of Defense and Veterans Affairs, EMDR blends seamlessly with other modalities to facilitate a dynamic and personalized healing experience that truly creates results.

</Faq>

<Faq question="I'm ashamed that I haven't been able to move past my trauma.">

Trauma responses aren’t a measure of your strength, character, or how hard you’ve tried to heal. Sometimes, understanding what happened isn’t enough to change the emotional and physical response associated with a memory. EMDR works with those unresolved responses rather than asking you to simply think your way out of them. Needing help doesn’t mean you’ve failed; it may simply mean you need a different way to process what happened.

</Faq>
