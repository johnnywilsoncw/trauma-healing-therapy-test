## About Alyssa

Every behavior is a message. When a child melts down, a teen shuts the bedroom door, or a family keeps landing in the same painful moments, someone is trying to communicate a need, often in the only way they know how.

You may be a parent who feels like you have tried everything, worn down by the daily battles and worried about what is really going on underneath them. You may be a teen who feels misunderstood, or a young adult stepping into a new chapter and unsure of your footing. Or you may be an adult beginning to notice how the patterns you grew up with, the ruptures, the losses, the things no one talked about, are still showing up in your relationships today.

I believe that kids, and people in general, do well if they can. When they can’t, it is not a matter of willpower. Something is getting in the way. Together, we get curious about what that something is: skills that are still developing, like communication, emotional regulation, flexibility, or connecting with others, or stress and trauma that has not yet had a safe place to land.

I especially love working with children (ages 5–12), teens (ages 13–17), and the parents and caregivers who love them, as well as families navigating change, stress, or trauma, new parents finding their way, and young adults entering new stages of life.

Therapy with me is a place where you will be met as a whole human, not a diagnosis or a problem to fix, and where joy and delight in your relationships can grow alongside healing.

### What It’s Like to Work Together

I am relational at my core. I bring my presence, curiosity, energy, culture, and humor into the room, and I adjust how much of myself I bring based on what you need. I take time to get to know how you communicate and what helps you feel comfortable, so our conversations feel both safe and thought-provoking.

Humor often shows up early in our work, when it fits. I like to say I am a first-time human, learning as I go, just like everyone else. I value humility and being genuine, which means owning my mistakes and repairing when something misses the mark. I want you to know you are not being judged or pathologized here. You are a person seeking support, and that takes courage.

I blend intuition with practical, analytical thinking. When you are facing a hard decision or wondering what comes next, I make space to explore your options and offer clear information about each one, so the choice you make is truly yours.

I also believe we can have hard conversations with kids. Divorce, trauma, loss, a new diagnosis, frightening world events: when children are not given honest, age-appropriate information, they fill in the gaps with their own stories, which are often more painful than the truth. Part of my work is helping families find the words.

Healing from trauma is not a straight line. It is more like a dance, with steps forward and steps back, and every one of them matters. Deeply rooted patterns take time to shift, so this work is best suited for people who are looking for real change rather than a quick fix. You do not need to have it all figured out to begin. Readiness can simply mean noticing that something is not working, for you or for the people you love, and being open to exploring what could be different.

## My Background & Experience

I have been practicing for more than 12 years in a wide range of settings, including school-based clinical roles, acute inpatient psychiatry for minors, and mandated outpatient substance use treatment. Across every setting, the foundation of my work has stayed the same: a trauma-informed approach grounded in safety, trust, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural humility.

I hold a certification in Trauma Play Therapy and have extensive training in Collaborative Problem Solving (CPS), Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), and Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT). Collaborative Problem Solving shapes how I understand challenges arising in client’s lives. I look at struggles through the lens of skill, not will, and focus on building the lagging skills that make everyday life harder. Trauma Play Therapy taught me how essential safety and security are before deeper healing can happen, especially for children.

![](../../assets/wp/2023/08/dr-mariella-lauriola-phd-services-emdr-image2.jpg)

One moment from my work stays close to me. I worked with a child and their parent for two years during late elementary school, supporting them through significant family stress. When we finished, the child moved on to middle school with strong coping skills and a steady family foundation. A few years later, the parent texted to ask if I would attend the child’s middle school graduation. I felt honored, and honestly a little surprised to still be remembered. I return to that moment often. It reminds me how meaningful this role can be in a child’s and a family’s story.

Two beliefs guide how I work. First, impact matters more than intent. Words carry weight, and listening and accountability are the first steps toward repair. Second, kids deserve honesty that meets them where they are developmentally.

Outside of sessions, I recharge with a good audiobook, a long walk, unscheduled time, and time with the people I love.
